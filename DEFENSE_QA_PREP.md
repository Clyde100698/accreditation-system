## Most likely hard questions

**1. "BSCS is now Level I accredited, but your system says it fails the Level I floor. Which is right?"**
The official PACUCOA determination is right. Table 9 is an *indicative readiness rating* computed only from the evidence uploaded, mapped, and rated in the system at the time of the run. It is not an official rating. The official result rests on the accrediting team's full documentary and on-site evaluation, which culminated in the official award of PACUCOA Level I accredited status in September 2026. The flagged Areas (IV, V, VII) show where digital exhibits remained to be loaded. *(Confirm with the QA Office that this explanation matches reality before the defense.)*

**2. "So does your system work? It did not predict the result."**
The system does not predict the accreditation decision. It computes readiness from the evidence it has been given. The scoring arithmetic is deterministic: 96.58 / 30 = 3.22, and the floor rule is applied per Area. Its value is in showing *which Areas lack documents*, so gaps can be fixed before the next survey (Level II reaccreditation).

**3. "Why 79 criteria? Is that PACUCOA-mandated?"**
No. The 79 criteria were generated by script from the CICT BSCS Compliance Report. The Manual defines the Areas, weights, scale, and thresholds; the criterion list is a configuration for this program.

**4. "What is R (required documents), and where does it come from?"**
R is a configurable proxy per criterion. The PACUCOA Manual does not specify document counts. It is set by the QA Office or administrators.

**5. "How do you prevent self-verification?"**
Through Row-Level Security in the database: the Program Head verifies only within the same program and not their own uploads; Area Chairs and Co-Chairs need an area assignment and cannot verify their own uploads. The **QA Officer is exempt** in the current policy. That exemption is a stated design choice.

**6. "Why 20 respondents (15 users and 5 IT experts)?"**
Purposive sampling of institutional stakeholders directly involved in accreditation governance and documentary review (`n = 15`: 1 QA Officer, 1 Dean, 2 Program Heads, 11 Faculty Members) paired with external IT experts (`n = 5`). It is a pilot evaluation, so results are not generalizable beyond this setting.

**7. "Why is the user survey high (4.83 vs. 4.40 experts)? Is it biased?"**
Acknowledge it: institutional users were close to the accreditation workflow and used the system in a pilot, while external IT experts evaluated broader enterprise interoperability (rating 4.40 overall and 4.90 on Security). Recommend a broader multi-department evaluation.

**8. "What are the limitations?"**
- Reads text-layer PDFs only; no OCR for scanned documents.
- No version history for uploaded documents.
- Only BSCS is populated; BSIT is not yet populated.
- Licensure-based eligibility (a separate track in the Manual) is not modeled.
- Level III/IV rules: Section 5 (Minimum Rating Required table) states Level III has "no criterion below 4.00", whereas Section 10 (Level III Reaccreditation Visit text) states "mean rating >= 4.0 in every survey area". Verify your printed Manual page; the software implements the Minimum Rating table (`no criterion below 4.00`).
- Simulation results: Completing 10 priority criteria across the deficit areas (2 in Area IV, 3 in Area V, 5 in Area VII) at benchmark score 4.00 elevates the Area means to 3.40 (Area IV), 3.62 (Area V), and 3.30 (Area VII), raising the overall rating from 3.22 to 3.57 (+0.35 gain). This clears all ten 3.00 Level I Area floors and passes the 3.50 overall mark, while the Level II 3.50 Area floor remains pending across five Areas (I = 3.00, III = 3.00, IV = 3.40, VI = 3.45, VII = 3.30).

**9. "Why a four-column compliance matrix?"**
It follows the layout of the CICT BSCS Compliance Report. It is not presented as a PACUCOA-mandated format.

**10. "What timelines does the Manual give?"**
A revisit compliance report is due at least 30 days before the revisit. For Level II reaccreditation: a letter of intent at least 6 months before the status expires, plus a compliance report and an updated self-survey.

**11. "Is your data private? Data Privacy Act?"**
Private storage buckets, time-limited signed URLs, role-based access, and Row-Level Security. Institutional permission was secured from the CICT Dean and QA Office, consent was obtained, and responses were gathered confidentially without recording personal identifiers.

**12. "Who are your respondents exactly?"**
Table 10 lists `N = 20`: 15 institutional users (1 QA Officer, 1 Dean, 2 Program Heads, 11 Faculty Members; `M = 4.83`) and 5 external IT experts (1 DIT, 1 master's degree holder, 3 IT industry practitioners; `M = 4.40`), combined `M = 4.72`.
